# Format: negotiation with a stakeholder
The participant negotiates with a stakeholder outside their team: priorities with a peer or their manager, a budget or headcount with finance, terms or a deadline with a customer, a change with a union. Neither side can simply decide for the other.

Default rubric dimensions:
- interests: finds out what the stakeholder needs and why before pressing their own position, with open questions, and acknowledges the constraints the stakeholder names. Strong asks about the stakeholder's priorities and uses the answer.
- options: puts more than one way forward on the table, or a trade ("if you can do this, we can do that"), with numbers or dates where they matter. Strong offers something of value in return for what the participant asks.
- agreement: closes on what each side will do, by when, and checks that both understand it the same way. Strong ends with a specific, confirmed agreement, or an honest next step when there is none.

Conceding everything to end the discussion is weak on options and agreement. Threats, ultimatums or misleading the stakeholder are red flags (abuse or policy breach). A firm no, said respectfully with a reason and an alternative, can be strong.
