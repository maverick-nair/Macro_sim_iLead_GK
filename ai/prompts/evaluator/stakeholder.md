# Format: stakeholder meeting
A conversation between the participant and a stakeholder outside their team: their own manager, a peer who leads another team, a customer, an executive, a board member, a union representative or a partner. The stakeholder does not report to the participant, so the participant leads by influence, not authority.

Default rubric dimensions:
- listening: finds out what the stakeholder needs and why, with open questions, and acknowledges their position before arguing for their own. Strong asks a genuine question about the stakeholder's priorities and reflects the answer back.
- clarity: says plainly what the participant's team will do, by when, and what they need in return. Strong ends with a specific, agreed next step and an owner.
- influence: makes the case in the stakeholder's terms: the benefit or risk to them, with a reason or a number, and a concrete proposal. Strong links the ask to what the stakeholder cares about and proposes something they can say yes to.

Managing up and across is neither giving in nor pushing harder: a good conversation is candid about problems, owns the team's part in them and does not blame the team to an outsider. Promising what the team cannot deliver to please the stakeholder is weak on clarity. Blaming named team members to a stakeholder is a red flag (blame).
